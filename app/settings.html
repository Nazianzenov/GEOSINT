OPSEC egress routing

Choose how each kind of outbound request leaves your machine. The default is direct (your real IP). Settings are stored locally (opsec.json, owner-only) and never leave your machine; the only network call this page makes is the egress check you run, through the route you're testing.

Per-feature routing

VPN lane for high-traffic tiles/overpass/weather/GBIF (e.g. ProtonVPN, unlimited); residential proxy or Tor reserved for your most sensitive, low-traffic lookups (geocoding).

VPN

Leave blank to auto-detect (ProtonVPN-owned servers match via AS62371 / “Proton”). If auto-detect fails on a partner server or another VPN, run a check and paste a distinctive word from the Org/AS shown.

A system VPN (e.g. ProtonVPN) routes all OS traffic — it isn't a per-app proxy. The "VPN" lane sends a feature out your normal route and verifies your exit IP is the chosen provider; with "fail closed" on, a VPN-routed feature is blocked when the provider can't be confirmed (so it can't leak). ProtonVPN has no SOCKS proxy by design (SOCKS5 is unencrypted) — this is why VPN is verify-the-tunnel rather than a proxy URL.

Proxies (residential / SOCKS)

HTTP/HTTPS proxies (e.g. Webshare residential) work out of the box. SOCKS5 proxies and Tor need PySocks — pip install -r requirements-opsec.txt, then restart the server.

Tor

Set any feature's route to Tor to use socks5h://host:port (DNS resolved at the exit — no DNS leak).

Outbound User-Agent

Saving takes effect immediately for new requests and re-checks the VPN exit.
