# Road markings & lines

Line **colour conventions are set nationally**, so the centre-line/edge-line colour pair is a
fast continental filter.

## Centre-line colour

| Centre line | Where |
|---|---|
| **Yellow** | USA, Canada, Mexico and most of Central/South America (per MUTCD and derivatives)[^mutcd]; China, South Korea; Norway; Chile, Argentina, Peru |
| **White** | Nearly all of Europe (Vienna Convention practice)[^vienna], Russia/CIS, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, most of Africa and SE Asia |
| **Mixed white/yellow** | Japan (yellow = no overtaking, white = normal) |

!!! tip "The Nordic trap"
    **Norway** keeps yellow centre lines; **Sweden, Denmark and (since 2020) Finland** use
    white. Yellow centre line + Nordic scenery → Norway (Finland's pre-2020 imagery still
    shows yellow, so check the Street View capture date).[^finland]

## Edge-line and special colours

- **Southern Africa** (South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Zimbabwe…): **yellow edge lines** with
  white centre lines — distinctive regional combo (SADC road-traffic-signs manual).[^sadc]
- **UK/Ireland**: yellow lines along the **kerb** mean parking restriction, not lane edge;
  double whites = no overtaking.[^ukhc]
- **Russia/CIS**: white edge; yellow used for no-stopping kerb marks and some centre lines on
  reversible lanes (GOST R 51256).[^gost]
- **Netherlands**: many rural roads have **no centre line** but broken edge lines
  (essentially a Dutch-only national policy, "60 km/h zones" per CROW design).[^crow]

## Pedestrian crossings

- European "zebra" stripes are near-universal, but **colour variants** localize:
  red-and-white zebras are common in **Switzerland (yellow!), Austria, parts of CEE**;
  Switzerland uniquely paints crossings **yellow**.
- US crosswalks: twin transverse lines or "continental" blocks vary by city (MUTCD options).[^mutcd]

[^mutcd]: FHWA, [Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices, Part 3 — Markings](https://mutcd.fhwa.dot.gov/) — yellow separates opposing traffic in the US system.
[^vienna]: UNECE, [Convention on Road Signs and Signals, Vienna 1968](https://unece.org/transport/road-traffic/vienna-convention-road-signs-and-signals) and the accompanying European supplement agreements — white as the standard carriageway marking colour.
[^finland]: Finnish Transport Infrastructure Agency (Väylävirasto), [Road Traffic Act 2020 reform — new road markings](https://vayla.fi/en/transport-network/roads/road-markings) — Finland's switch from yellow to white centre lines effective June 2020.
[^sadc]: SADC, *Road Traffic Signs Manual* (used by South Africa per SANRAL); see South Africa's [National Road Traffic Act regulations](https://www.gov.za/documents/national-road-traffic-act).
[^ukhc]: UK Government, [The Highway Code — road markings](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/road-markings).
[^gost]: GOST R 51256-2018, *Traffic management devices. Road markings* (Russian national standard).
[^crow]: CROW (NL), *Handboek wegontwerp* — essential-recognisability marking policy on 60 km/h rural roads; summarised by [SWOV fact sheets](https://swov.nl/en/fact-sheet/road-design).
